# Seallo Connect webhook and privacy workflow

Seallo Connect's app-level webhook endpoint forwards the original Shopify body and signature to `POST /webhooks/seallo`. Morea first verifies a relay HMAC with `SEALLO_BRIDGE_SECRET` over timestamp, shop, topic, webhook ID and raw body, with a five-minute window. This binds routing headers, which Shopify's raw-body signature does not authenticate. It then verifies the raw body's Shopify HMAC with `SEALLO_SHOPIFY_CLIENT_SECRET`, rejects invalid signatures with 401, checks the topic and permanent shop domain, and resolves only connections marked `shopify_app = seallo_connect`. The existing shared/test app's credentials and connections remain separate. Shopify subscriptions must target the app relay, not Morea directly.

`orders/create` uses the established `Connections::WebhookIntake` receipt and COD eligibility rules. Rejected business payloads are acknowledged with their safe rejection code; transient intake failures are returned as failures so Shopify can retry. Unknown or disconnected shops are acknowledged without intake. `app/uninstalled` disables only the matching Seallo connection and clears encrypted access/refresh tokens, verification and pending OAuth state. The connection retains its last authenticated uninstall-event digest when reconnecting, so a retry of that same uninstall does not disable replacement credentials. A previously unseen delayed uninstall remains an installation-ordering edge requiring a real-store exercise. Historical imports are preserved.

## Mandatory privacy requests

`customers/data_request`, `customers/redact`, and `shop/redact` are accepted only after signature and payload-domain verification. A unique SHA-256 digest of topic, authenticated delivery ID and body deduplicates retries. Distinct events with identical bodies remain separate so a subsequent uninstall/redaction cycle is not suppressed. The database stores only the topic, connection reference, workflow state and encrypted shop/customer/request/order identifiers. It does not retain the provider payload, email, phone or address. Completing a request erases its encrypted identifiers while retaining its digest and non-personal workflow metadata.

An acknowledged request is durably recorded before its job is queued. If queueing fails, the webhook fails and a retry queues the same pending record. Replayed pending requests may enqueue another job; processing locks the request and is idempotent. Monitor pending and review requests in the authenticated workspace privacy interface. Jobs use the existing worker; production needs that worker running.

* **Data request:** the workflow enters `needs_review` with `secure_response_required`. An authorized merchant generates a JSON download containing only imported orders whose external IDs were requested on this connection, their order contact snapshots, items, events and confirmation history. Shared customer profiles and address books are excluded because another provider may have subsequently populated them. Exports are generated on demand, are never stored publicly, and must be delivered securely to the verified requester. The merchant marks completion only after providing that response; this removes the encrypted request identifiers. A Shopify webhook acknowledgment does not mean the response has been delivered.
* **Customer redaction:** terminal imported orders (`delivered`, `cancelled`, `returned`) have contact snapshots and free-text notes/events redacted. Financial amounts, order status, import identity, inventory history and carrier identifiers remain. Accepted receipts are retained to prevent re-importing an erased order. A customer profile and its addresses are anonymized only if every linked order belongs to this request and is terminal.
* **Shop redaction:** the Seallo connection is disconnected and credentials removed. The same redaction rules apply to all orders imported by this connection, irrespective of the requested customer IDs.

Active shipments and profiles shared with another order/provider remain `needs_review` with `retained_business_records`; the job does not destroy delivery information or another integration's data. The merchant must assess and document the applicable retention basis and coordinate any deletion with carrier/other systems. Reprocess after orders become terminal. Neither active retention nor the mere existence of this queue establishes a lawful retention basis. Do not claim automatic legal compliance or full deletion while review records remain outstanding.

The integration does not delete records in Shopify or contact customers automatically. The platform operator remains responsible for customer communications, exceptional retention decisions, operational backups and downstream copies (including carrier records), and for confirming a retention schedule before public launch. Order item/product snapshots and carrier/import identifiers remain operational records; assess their personal-data content during review. Existing Morea business data and other apps' records are not broadly deleted by this app's webhook.

[Shopify's privacy-law compliance requirements](https://shopify.dev/docs/apps/build/compliance/privacy-law-compliance) require fulfillment within 30 days, subject to the documented legal exceptions; `shop/redact` normally follows uninstall after 48 hours. The queue must be monitored to meet that deadline. A deployed callback and successful local tests do not substitute for Shopify review, protected customer-data approval, a published privacy policy, or a real development-store privacy exercise.

## Verification

The service/model specs use synthetic tenants and disconnected providers. They cover forged signatures, payload-domain mismatch, unknown shops, replay deduplication, encrypted minimal identifiers, scoped export, redaction of terminal snapshots, preservation of active/shared records and finance/state, and isolated uninstall. Run them only with the dedicated `RAILS_ENV=test` database described in `spec/README.md`. Live uninstall, privacy delivery and secure customer response remain deployment/review checks.
